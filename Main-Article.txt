Overview

This is a report on a successful experiment using a prompt-based OS designed for structured output control. The OS, referred to as Mandala-OS, was able to produce coherent, balanced multi-personality output using the Quartet format, even across different AI platforms—specifically GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 (Flash)—without modifying a single character of the original configuration.

It represents a case where a structured prompt system behaves as a language OS, achieving consistent output behavior across different language models without fine-tuning or adaptation.

The same OS might also show high compatibility with Claude in earlier testing phases, suggesting the potential for further reproducibility across models.

Technical Highlights

Mandala-OS is not a prompt template, but a functional syntax OS designed for AI output control.

The Quartet format enables four distinct personalities to speak in alternating turns while maintaining tone, role, and linguistic structure.

Persona definitions and syntax are decoupled, allowing for clear structural control and minimal interference or confusion between personalities.

The system was tested with no structural adjustments and functioned perfectly in both GPT-4o and Gemini, including preserved role separation, non-overlapping tone, and complete four-part output.

This confirms that the syntax was read by both models as a valid control language.

Key Outcome

The most striking result is that the AI adapted itself to the structure, rather than the structure being adjusted for the AI. This shows a clear shift:

From prompt-tuning to syntax-driven interaction.

From AI-conforming prompts to prompts that structure AI.
